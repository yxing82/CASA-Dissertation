Cascading Displacement in London

Three-column alluvial showing how 983 London MSOAs flow from their IMD Rank Decile 2010 through a gentrification classification to their IMD Rank Decile 2019. Deciles are derived from mean LSOA IMD rank aggregated to MSOA level (1 = most deprived, 10 = least deprived). Census O-D data from 2011 & 2021 defines cascade flows; IMD percentile change validates the classification.

Hover or click a category in the middle column to see inflow / outflow breakdown

Aggregate cascade flows by IMD rank decile — showing inflow from wealthier areas (higher-ranked) and outflow to poorer areas (lower-ranked), across both census periods.

Reading the pattern

Deciles 1–5 (most deprived) show net positive cascade — more people arriving from higher-ranked areas than leaving to lower-ranked ones. This is the gentrification signal. Deciles 6–10 show the mirror: net negative cascade, as outflows to lower-ranked areas exceed inflows from higher. The symmetry pivot around Decile 5 is the clearest fingerprint of the cascading displacement mechanism, persisting across both census periods.

Data: ONS Census Origin-Destination 2011 & 2021 · IMD 2010 & 2019 (MHCLG) · 983 London MSOAs · Deciles from mean LSOA IMD rank aggregated to MSOA (1 = most deprived, 10 = least deprived) · Classification: cascade direction (Net Cascade) × IMD rank decile shift (2010→2019)
