Dissertation analysis pipeline: seven stages from raw data acquisition through visualisation and reporting.

▸ Stage 1 Data acquisition
Census origin–destination migration data
2011: Table MF01EW — safeguarded OA-level flows from WICID (3.7M rows, 3 cols: origin OA, dest OA, persons)
2021: Table RM097 — MSOA-level flows from NOMIS (1.49M rows, 5 cols including origin/dest MSOA codes + migrant count)
Index of Multiple Deprivation
IMD 2010: LSOA-level scores and ranks (32,482 LSOAs) — used as fixed baseline hierarchy
IMD 2019: LSOA-level scores, ranks, and mid-2015 population estimates (32,844 LSOAs) — used for validation only
Geographic lookups
ONS postcode lookup (NSPCL NOV22) for OA→MSOA mapping (232,044 unique OA–MSOA pairs)
ONS MSOA 2011-to-2021 correspondence table (7,201 rows)
Sources: WICID, NOMIS, GOV.UK, Open Geography Portal
↓
▸ Stage 2 Spatial harmonisation & data processing
2a. Geography harmonisation
2021 O-D: Map MSOA 2021 codes → 2011 codes via correspondence table. Retain only unchanged 1:1 MSOAs (7,080 of 7,201). Split/merged MSOAs excluded.
2011 O-D: Map OA codes → 2011 MSOA codes via postcode lookup. No cross-year translation needed — native 2011 geography.
2b. IMD aggregation (LSOA → MSOA)
Population-weighted averaging of LSOA IMD scores/ranks to 2011 MSOA boundaries.
Weights: mid-2015 LSOA population from IMD 2019 release, applied to both 2010 and 2019 IMD.
Rank → percentile normalisation: rank / n_lsoa to ensure cross-release comparability.
2c. London scope & analysis whitelist
Filter to 33 London local authority districts (32 boroughs + City of London).
Analysis whitelist: 983 MSOAs with valid IMD scores for both 2010 and 2019. A flow is retained iff both endpoints are in the whitelist.
2d. Endpoint classification & exclusion audit
Every O-D code classified into mutually exclusive categories: london_analysis, ew_non_london, boundary_changed, scotland, n_ireland, special_code (−8, 999999999, OD-prefixed), unknown.
Whitelist-based filtering (positive definition). Per-reason waterfall counts exported for methodology reporting.
2e. Flow table construction
2011 OA flows aggregated to MSOA-to-MSOA level (groupby origin_msoa × dest_msoa → sum persons).
Intra-MSOA moves (origin = destination) removed — they represent local churn, not inter-neighbourhood restructuring.
Retained: 2011: ~5.1M migrants across ~1.37M MSOA pairs; 2021: ~1.35M flows (90.5% endpoint mapping rate).
Notebook: data_preprocess_od_drop_out_logic_rewrite_20260612.ipynb
↓
▸ Stage 3 Deprivation hierarchy & flow classification
3a. Fixed baseline deprivation hierarchy
Each of 983 London MSOAs assigned a wealth decile based on IMD 2010 scores (via pd.qcut(IMD_2010, 10), inverted so Decile 1 = most deprived).
Classification is fixed and applied to both 2011 and 2021 flows. This separates measurement of migration restructuring from measurement of neighbourhood outcomes.
3b. Directional flow classification
For each O-D flow, compare origin and destination deciles:
Cascade flows: Inflow_Wealthier = migrants arriving from higher decile (less deprived) origins; Outflow_Poorer = migrants leaving to lower decile (more deprived) destinations
Counter-cascade flows: Outflow_Wealthier = leaving to higher decile; Inflow_Poorer = arriving from lower decile
Lateral flows: same-decile moves (Decile_Shift = 0) — excluded from cascade metrics but retained for total migration normalisation
3c. Four-flow consistency check
Verification: Inflow_Wealthier + Inflow_Poorer + Inflow_Lateral = Total_Inflow (and same for outflows).
Zero negative lateral residuals confirms mutually exclusive and exhaustive decomposition.
↓
▸ Stage 4 CFI metric construction
Four cascading flow index metrics (per MSOA, per census period)
Net Cascade = Inflow_Wealthier − Outflow_Poorer — directional balance (positive = net upward inflows)
CFI Churn = Inflow_Wealthier + Outflow_Poorer — total cascade intensity regardless of direction
CFI Rate = (Inflow_W × Outflow_P) / Total_Migration — normalised co-occurrence (high only when both flows elevated simultaneously)
% Inflow Wealthier = (Inflow_W / Total_Inflow) × 100 — compositional share of inflows from less deprived areas
Temporal change indicators
Delta metrics computed: Δ = metric_21 − metric_11 for each CFI metric to capture restructuring dynamics across the decade.
Output: msoa_cascade_features_{date}.csv — 983 rows × 33 columns
↓
▸ Stage 5 Restructuring typology
Two-stage classification (from 2011 baseline thresholds)
Step 1 — Intensity split: CFI Churn median divides MSOAs into low-churn (→ Stable) and high-churn (→ proceed to Step 2).
Step 2 — Directional split (high-churn only): Net Cascade quartiles among high-churn MSOAs:
  • Upper quartile (Q75+) → Ascent-dominated — strong upward inflows, socio-economic upgrading signature
  • Interquartile range → Cascading circulation — high churn, balanced directionality, continuous flow-through
  • Lower quartile (Q25−) → Displacement-dominated — strong downward outflows, displacement pressure
Alternative typology formulations explored in EDA
Temporal typologies (Emerging / Sustained / Stalled / Stable) based on period-specific median thresholds for CFI Churn, CFI Rate, and Net Cascade — cross-tabulated to assess convergence.
↓
▸ Stage 6 Validation & sensitivity analysis
6a. Primary validation outcome
IMD Percentile Change = IMD_Pctile_2019 − IMD_Pctile_2010. Positive = relative upward repositioning (became less deprived). Secondary: raw IMD score change (interpreted cautiously due to cross-release non-comparability).
6b. Bivariate correlation analysis
Pearson r between each CFI metric and IMD Percentile Change.
Key finding: CFI Churn shows strongest correlation with percentile change (r ≈ +0.38); CFI Rate robust secondary (r ≈ +0.24). Net Cascade correlates more strongly with score change (r ≈ −0.44) but not percentile change.
6c. Partial correlation analysis
Controlling for IMD 2010 baseline scores to check whether relationships persist independently of initial deprivation intensity.
6d. Typological validation (ANOVA)
One-way ANOVA of mean IMD Percentile Change across the four restructuring regimes.
CFI Churn typology produces strongest separation (F = 38.76, p < 0.001). Ascent-dominated MSOAs average highest percentile gains.
6e. IMD baseline sensitivity analysis
Full re-estimation of all metrics under alternative 2019 IMD classification (4 combinations: 2011/2021 OD × 2010/2019 IMD baseline).
Tests robustness via: cross-baseline Pearson r (expected > 0.80), validation concordance, decile assignment stability, borough-level CFI Rate concordance.
Notebooks: eda_cascade_imd_rank_analysis.ipynb, sensitivity_imd_baseline_20260526.ipynb

▸ Stage 7 Visualisation & reporting
Three-column alluvial diagram
Self-contained HTML visualisation: Wealth Decile 2010 → Restructuring Typology → Wealth Decile 2019.
Shows how MSOAs flow from baseline deprivation position through cascade regime to outcome position. Includes decile breakdown view with inflow/outflow bars per decile for both census periods.
EDA outputs
Distribution comparisons (2011 vs 2021 overlay histograms), correlation heatmaps (14×14 matrix), scatter plots with regression fits, typology comparison charts, borough-level CFI Rate analysis, partial correlation tables.
Script: cascade_alluvial_restructure_imd_corroboration.py
Key design principles
Separation of measurement and outcome: CFI metrics are derived entirely from migration flow asymmetries; IMD change enters only at the validation stage, avoiding conceptual circularity.
Fixed baseline: The 2010 IMD hierarchy is frozen across both census periods so that changes in metric values reflect genuine shifts in migration structures, not reclassification artefacts.
Whitelist-based filtering: Retained flows are defined positively (both endpoints resolve to a London MSOA with a wealth decile), preventing unanticipated codes from leaking into the analysis.
Unit of analysis: 983 temporally consistent MSOAs across Greater London (after excluding split/merged boundaries).
