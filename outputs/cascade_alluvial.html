Cascading Displacement in London

Three-column alluvial showing how 983 London MSOAs flow from their 2010 IMD decile through a flow-based restructuring typology to their 2019 IMD decile. Classification uses CFI Churn (median threshold) to identify active restructuring, then Net Cascade quartiles to distinguish ascent-dominated, displacement-dominated, and cascading circulation regimes.

Aggregate cascade flows by IMD rank decile — showing inflow from wealthier areas (higher-ranked) and outflow to poorer areas (lower-ranked), across both census periods. Inspired by the Nomis census flow tool.

Reading the pattern

Deciles 1–5 (most deprived) show net positive cascade — more people arriving from wealthier areas than leaving to poorer ones, consistent with ascent-dominated restructuring. Deciles 6–10 show the mirror: net negative cascade, as outflows to poorer areas exceed inflows from wealthier, indicating displacement-dominated dynamics. The pattern persists across both census periods, though cascade volumes decreased slightly between 2011 and 2021. The symmetry pivot around Decile 5 is the clearest fingerprint of the cascading displacement mechanism.

Data: ONS Census Origin-Destination 2011 & 2021 · IMD 2010 & 2019 (MHCLG) · 983 London MSOAs · Wealth deciles from mean LSOA-to-MSOA IMD scores · Classification: CFI Churn (median threshold) × Net Cascade direction (quartile thresholds among high-churn MSOAs)
